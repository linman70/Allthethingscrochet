Welcome to Amigurumi

Amigurumi is the Japanese art of crocheting or knitting small stuffed toys and characters. Learn the basics, explore construction techniques and make a little friend of your own.

Try the beginner pattern

1. What makes amigurumi special?

Amigurumi projects are usually worked in continuous rounds to create a firm fabric that holds stuffing. Most designs are built from simple shapes—spheres, tubes, cones and ovals—that are joined together to form animals, food, fantasy creatures and charming everyday objects. Small details such as embroidered faces, ears, tails and accessories give each character its personality.

2. Essential tools and techniques

To begin, gather smooth yarn, a crochet hook that creates a fairly tight fabric, stitch markers, stuffing, scissors and a yarn needle. Learn the magic ring, single crochet, increases and invisible decreases. Place a marker in the first stitch of each round so you can keep track of where the round begins. Stuff gradually and evenly; too much stuffing can stretch the stitches and show through the fabric.

3. Giving your amigurumi character

Personality comes from the small choices: a color palette, the angle of the ears, a tiny scarf or a stitched smile. Before sewing parts permanently, pin them in place and check the shape from several angles. Keep facial features aligned and securely attached. If the toy is for a young child, embroidered features are generally a safer choice than hard plastic pieces, and all parts should be secured appropriately.

Mini Amigurumi Ball

BeginnerUS terms

A simple, round practice toy that teaches the magic ring, increases, even rounds and decreases. Add embroidered eyes and a smile to turn it into a tiny character.

Finished size: about 2.5–3 in, depending on yarn and tension.

Materials

  • Small amount of smooth worsted-weight yarn
  • 3.5 mm crochet hook (use a smaller hook if gaps show)
  • Polyester stuffing, stitch marker, yarn needle and scissors
  • Black embroidery thread for the face

Abbreviations

MR = magic ring · sc = single crochet · inc = 2 sc in one stitch · dec = invisible decrease · st = stitch

Directions — work in continuous rounds

  1. Round 1: 6 sc in MR (6).
  2. Round 2: inc in each st around (12).
  3. Round 3: [sc, inc] around (18).
  4. Round 4: [2 sc, inc] around (24).
  5. Rounds 5–8: sc in each st around (24).
  6. Round 9: [2 sc, dec] around (18).
  7. Round 10: [sc, dec] around (12). Stuff firmly but do not overfill.
  8. Round 11: dec around (6). Fasten off, close the opening and weave in the end.

Finish the face

Use embroidery thread to stitch two small eyes and a smile. Secure and hide all thread ends inside the ball. For a child’s toy, embroider all features rather than attaching small hard pieces.

Tip: Keep your stitches tight and use a stitch marker at the beginning of every round. The stitch count at the end of each round is shown in parentheses.
