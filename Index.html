A brief history of crochet

Crochet is made by pulling loops of yarn or thread through other loops with a hooked tool. The precise origins of the craft are difficult to establish: historians have proposed several possible roots, but clear written and visual evidence becomes more common in Europe during the 19th century. Crochet became especially popular in the Victorian era, when patterns appeared in magazines and instruction books and makers used the technique for lace, clothing and decorative home items. Over time, crochet spread around the world and developed into a wonderfully varied craft—from traditional lacework to modern garments, blankets and amigurumi.

Advantages of crocheting

Crocheting is creative, portable and adaptable. It can help you slow down, focus on a hands-on task and enjoy the satisfaction of making something useful or beautiful. It offers room to experiment with color, texture and design, and it can be a social activity when you share ideas with other makers. Crochet projects can also make personal gifts and allow you to use leftover yarn. Most importantly, you can choose a project that suits your time, budget and skill level—one small square or stitch at a time.
